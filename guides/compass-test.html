What is the COMPASS pilot aptitude test?

COMPASS stands for Computerized Pilot Aptitude Screening System and is an EPST product. EPST describes six core tests and says prior flying experience is not required. Its page also lists technical comprehension, aviation English and verbal reasoning. Confirm the battery, equipment and duration in your own invitation.

The six core areas listed by EPST
TestSkill assessed
ControlHand, foot and eye coordination.
SlalomHand–eye tracking.
Task managerScreen scanning and concurrent task management.
MemoryShort-term recall.
MathematicsApplied mathematical reasoning and speed.
OrientationInstrument interpretation and spatial understanding.

A useful first step is to make a checklist from your invitation: which tasks, which controls, which language, and what familiarisation is provided. A mobile drill can support a practice habit, but it cannot reproduce the feel of the assessment’s physical controls.

How should you interpret COMPASS results?

Ask the assessing organisation how it reports results and what its selection requirements are. Do not convert a practice-app level or an internet percentage into an official pass mark. Keep your practice results as a record of your own accuracy, pace and recurring mistakes.

Original practice examples with worked answers

Try these original practice examples before opening each explanation. Review the method, then try a fresh example using the same approach.

1. Speed, distance and time

An aircraft covers 150 nautical miles in 50 minutes at a constant groundspeed. What is its groundspeed in knots?

Show answer and method

180 knots. One knot is one nautical mile per hour. Convert the time: 50 minutes = 5/6 hour. Then 150 ÷ (5/6) = 180. Alternatively, 150 ÷ 50 = 3 nautical miles per minute; multiply by 60. Check that a journey taking less than an hour gives a speed greater than 150 knots.

2. A heading change across north

Starting on a heading of 350°, turn right by 40°. What is the new heading?

Show answer and method

030°. A right turn increases the heading: 350 + 40 = 390. Subtract 360 to wrap around north: 30°. Write headings with three digits. A sketch of north, east, south and west can expose a left/right error before you practise mentally.

3. Recall after a distraction

Read the sequence 7 – 2 – 9 – 4 – 1 once. Cover it, calculate 18 + 27, then write the sequence in its original order.

Show answer and method

The calculation is 45; the sequence is 7 – 2 – 9 – 4 – 1. Record whether you lost an item or swapped its position. For the next attempt use a new sequence. Repeating a memorised answer would measure familiarity with that example rather than recall of new information.

A suggested 14-day COMPASS preparation plan

This is our practice schedule, not an EPST prescription. Adjust it to your invitation and available time. A 20–25 minute session can include two short drills and an error review; stop when fatigue makes the review unproductive.

Practice schedule
WhenWhat to doWhat to review
Days 1–2Read the provider instructions. Try each available familiarisation exercise.Write down the rules and identify two weak areas.
Days 3–5Practise arithmetic and heading changes, initially without a timer.Explain every wrong answer; distinguish a method error from a rushed response.
Days 6–8Alternate fresh memory items, spatial exercises and coordination practice.Keep difficulty and controls consistent when comparing sessions.
Days 9–11Add short timed rounds and a combined-attention drill.Record both missed signals and incorrect reactions.
Days 12–13Return to the official familiarisation and review your error log.Check that you can follow instructions without relying on memorised examples.
Day 14Use light review and check travel, documents and the test time.Arrive rested; avoid a last-minute score chase.

Keep a simple practice log: date, exercise, difficulty, correct answers, time and one mistake to fix. Compare sessions with the same settings. Use the log to choose what to practise next.

Frequently asked questions

Where should I start preparing for COMPASS?

Start with the flight school’s invitation and any familiarisation it provides. Use EPST’s overview to understand the test families, then practise the skills you find difficult with new examples.

Can I practise for COMPASS on an iPhone?

Yes. Brain Preflight offers arithmetic, memory, spatial and divided-attention exercises on iPhone, with five free practice sessions per day and optional paid Premium.

What is the COMPASS pass mark or retake waiting period?

Confirm both with the organisation assessing you. This guide does not give a universal cut-off or waiting period, because those details need to match your specific selection.

How does Brain Preflight track progress?

The app uses its own 1–9 practice levels, session history and progress views. Compare sessions on the same exercise and settings to decide what to practise next.
