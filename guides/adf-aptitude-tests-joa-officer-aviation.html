Job Opportunities Assessment (JOA)

ADF Careers describes the JOA as general ability and mathematical ability questions. Its candidate guide, revised on 21 September 2026, covers numerical, verbal and abstract reasoning: 51 questions in 20 minutes after a familiarisation section. The guide allows up to three attempts, six months apart.

The same guide says study is unnecessary, suggests you avoid other practice websites, and says the ADF does not recommend or endorse any course or website. Start with the official guide and its examples. Brain Preflight is independent general skill practice; it does not promise a better result.

Officer Aviation selection

For the Air Force Officer Aviation role, ADF Careers describes a primary selection with interviews and an initial Officer Aviation Test Battery (OATB). Suitable candidates then attend a two-day Aviation Screening Program at RAAF Base East Sale, where aviation aptitude is assessed with the computer-based MACTS over two four-hour sessions. An Officer Selection Board may follow, with group activities, problem-solving exercises and an interview. The public page does not list the OATB or MACTS subtests.

Provisional routine: open Logic sequences, Calculation grid, Cube nets, Stacks, 3D objects, Shapes & colors, Psychomotor and Airways. It gives general practice in reasoning, calculation, spatial, attention and concurrent-task skills. It is not an OATB or MACTS simulation, and touchscreen tracking does not reproduce test equipment. Board exercises and interviews need separate preparation.

FAQ

Do I need to study for the ADF JOA?

The ADF says study is unnecessary and points candidates to its own guide. Use that first. Brain Preflight is optional general practice for numerical and logical skills.

Does Brain Preflight simulate MACTS?

No. MACTS subtests are not published, and the app’s touchscreen exercises do not reproduce its equipment or scoring.
