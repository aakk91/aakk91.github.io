What is RAF CBAT, and is it different from DAA?

CBAT means Computer Based Aptitude Tests. The RAF pilot role page describes a separate assessment at the Officers and Aircrew Selection Centre at RAF College Cranwell. It identifies physical responses to visual information, two-dimensional to three-dimensional reasoning, simultaneous tasks and other reasoning skills. Read your own role page and invitation for the process that applies to you.

The same RAF role page distinguishes the aptitude-test stage from CBAT. Do not assume that finishing DAA preparation covers the aircrew assessment. The UK selection guide also separates Army ACT from both RAF routes.

Can I try original CBAT-related skill examples?

The following examples illustrate mental arithmetic and spatial reasoning. They are our own teaching examples and are not official CBAT questions or a sample of the complete assessment.

Mental arithmetic: a vehicle travels 36 km in 24 minutes at a constant speed. What is the speed in km/h?

24 minutes is 0.4 hours. Speed = distance ÷ time = 36 ÷ 0.4 = 90 km/h. Checking the time unit before dividing avoids the common error of treating minutes as hours.

Spatial reasoning: a cube has red opposite blue, green opposite yellow, and white opposite black. Can red, blue and white meet at one corner?

No. Red and blue are opposite faces, so they cannot share an edge or meet at the same corner. Opposite-face relationships are a useful starting check before trying to visualise a full rotation.

For repeated spatial work, open Object rotation or Cube nets. For a different pilot-selection context, the COMPASS guide and TBAS guide explain where related app skills fit; neither is a replacement for RAF instructions.

How should I build a CBAT skills practice routine?

Begin with one task at a time so you can identify the source of an error. Try arithmetic, a spatial exercise and a memory session. Use the remaining allowance to review the weakest skill or try a Psychomotor session. When handling several tasks, note which task you neglected and whether an instruction change caused the problem.

  • Read and follow the instructions for each exercise rather than relying on a familiar pattern.
  • Review accuracy as well as speed; a rushed response can hide a misunderstood rule.
  • Practise concurrent tasks after understanding each component separately.
  • Plan the next session around a specific recurring error.
  • Follow the arrival, travel and preparation information sent by the RAF for your actual appointment.

This is an editorial routine with no guaranteed score effect or prescribed preparation duration. The practice questions and answers guide explains free daily access and how to interpret app levels.

Does the app reproduce the real CBAT or predict a pass?

No. Brain Preflight’s CBAT programme is a skills routine using independent exercises. Touchscreen tracking does not reproduce official controls, and app levels do not convert into RAF aptitude scores. The app’s timed series are its own practice format, not a complete CBAT sitting.

Use official RAF guidance for your role’s eligibility, assessment arrangements and other recruitment stages. Interviews, leadership and teamwork assessment, medical checks and fitness preparation require separate attention. Ask your recruiter about result requirements or any retest arrangements rather than relying on a general website’s pass-mark claim.

If your invitation names DAA, use the Defence Aptitude Assessment guide. If it names a civilian airline assessment, start with the cut-e / Aon guide or the relevant provider-specific page instead.
