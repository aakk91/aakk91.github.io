CFAT replaced by SEAF: Canadian Forces application guide (2026)

The Canadian Forces Aptitude Test (CFAT, or TACF in French) was replaced by the Scored Employment Application Form (SEAF) in October 2024. This guide explains the change and distinguishes it from aircrew aptitude testing. Older CFAT guides can describe a previous process

The Royal Military College’s 2025–2026 recruitment brochure states that the Canadian Armed Forces replaced the Canadian Forces Aptitude Test (CFAT) with the Scored Employment Application Form (SEAF) in October 2024. The Department of National Defence describes the SEAF as a scored application form covering education, experience, leadership and other life achievements. It is not a timed cognitive test. Do not assume an old CFAT practice booklet describes the steps in a new application.

Check the instructions for your own application

Before paying for a course or scheduling weeks of test practice, confirm the assessment named in your recruitment instructions. Ask your recruiting centre if the wording is unclear. A practice app does not establish whether you must sit a particular test. The SEAF should not be presented as a renamed version of a timed cognitive battery.

Aircrew selection is a separate process

The Canadian Forces Aircrew Selection Centre (CFASC) tests Pilot, Aerospace Control and Air Combat Systems Officer candidates. Its candidate guide, modified on 14 January 2026, describes about seven hours of timed, computer-based aptitude tests over two days, assessing cognitive and psychomotor abilities. Some tests use a joystick, a foot pedal or headphones. Older pages call this battery CFAST; the current candidate guide does not use that name. Aircrew testing is distinct from the former CFAT and from the SEAF.

What general practice can and cannot do

Brain Preflight includes mental arithmetic, reasoning, spatial and memory exercises. These can be used for general practice, but they do not complete a SEAF application, and touchscreen exercises do not reproduce the CFASC tests or equipment. Brain Preflight’s Canadian Armed Forces programme is general aptitude practice. Earlier versions labelled it CFAT; that label does not indicate a current recruiting test.
