The FEAST test for air traffic controllers, explained

FEAST (First European Air Traffic Controller Selection Test) is the EUROCONTROL-developed battery used by air navigation service providers across Europe and beyond to screen ATC candidates. Compared to the pilot side, there is remarkably little prep material about it. Here is what it measures and how to get ready.

How the selection is structured

Most FEAST-using organisations run it in phases. The first phase is a cognitive battery, several hours at a test centre. Candidates who pass move to a second phase built around work-sample tasks that feel like simplified ATC: keeping simulated traffic apart while instructions and interruptions arrive. A personality questionnaire and English assessment sit somewhere in the chain depending on the provider. The cut-offs and exact module mix belong to each organisation, not to FEAST itself.

What phase 1 measures

Visual perception and monitoring

Spotting targets, changes and conflicts across a busy display. Rewards a scan cycle, punishes staring.

Spatial orientation

Headings, relative bearings, rotated reference frames, answered quickly. Controllers live in this space all day; the test checks you can too.

Short-term and working memory

Holding callsigns, numbers and instructions while new items keep arriving, then retrieving them accurately under time pressure.

Timed mental arithmetic

Speeds, levels, rates and simple sums on a short clock, the same mechanic every aviation battery uses.

Planning and multitasking

The heart of the job and the hardest part of the test: several streams demanding attention at once, with priorities that shift. Untrained candidates collapse here first, and it is also where training moves scores fastest.

English comprehension

Listening and understanding under realistic conditions, since English is the working language.

The overlap with pilot selection batteries

FEAST and the pilot batteries (COMPASS, cut-e, PILAPT) are cousins: tracking or monitoring, timed maths, memory sequences, spatial orientation and a combined multitask section appear on both sides. One controller who sat both COMPASS and FEAST put it simply: the first was practice for the second. If you find FEAST-specific material thin, pilot-battery prep trains the same underlying mechanics.

Preparing

  1. Train divided attention deliberately. The multitask and planning sections dominate the outcome. Combined practice only: monitor while you compute.
  2. Automate timed arithmetic. Daily short drills; rates and sums should cost no thinking budget.
  3. Build the scan habit. Practise catching peripheral changes while working a central task.
  4. Protect your sleep before test day. A several-hour battery measures your worst hour, not your best.

FAQ

Can I retake FEAST?

Retake rules belong to each organisation, and many enforce long waiting periods or a limited number of lifetime attempts. Check your provider's policy before you book a first attempt casually.

Do I need aviation knowledge?

Phase 1 assumes none. It measures aptitude: perception, memory, spatial reasoning, arithmetic speed and multitasking. The job knowledge comes later in training.

Is FEAST the same everywhere?

The core battery is shared, but each organisation picks modules, sets cut-offs and adds its own phases, so accounts from candidates at different providers can differ in the details.
