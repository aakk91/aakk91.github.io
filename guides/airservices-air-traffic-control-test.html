What Airservices says it measures

After you apply, Airservices asks you to complete online ability tests. Its air traffic control careers FAQ says they measure your ability to follow written instructions, your numerical and verbal reasoning, your spatial awareness, and whether you can work with complicated patterns and check data. The FAQ does not name a test provider, so do not assume it is FEAST or the FAA’s ATSA.

A routine for the app

Open these exercises from the catalogue: Same or not, Logic sequences, Calculation grid, Cube nets, Stacks, 3D objects and Shapes & colors. Airways and Psychomotor are optional general multitasking practice; the FAQ does not list them as test sections. Use other material for verbal reasoning and written instructions. Online testing is one step of selection, so follow the instructions Airservices sends for each later stage.

Is there a free Airservices ATC practice app?

Brain Preflight is free on iPhone with 5 sessions every day. It practises data checking, spatial, numerical and pattern skills named in the Airservices FAQ. It has no verbal reasoning or written-instruction exercise.
